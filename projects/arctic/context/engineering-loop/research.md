# Air-conditioning service-management requirements

Research checked 2026-10-05 against public field-service product documentation and manufacturer / U.S. Department of Energy maintenance references. Vendor pages document common product workflows, not independent proof that every feature is necessary for every contractor. Technical maintenance advice is equipment- and jurisdiction-specific.

## Core operating requirements

| ID | Requirement | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Keep each customer, service site, contact, and one-or-more installed units linked to that site's service history. Record the unit identity and model/serial details when known, then retain prior jobs, findings, parts, quotes, invoices, and next-service dates. | Commercial sites may have multiple assets, and the arriving technician needs the right equipment and prior work context. |
| R2 | Capture the service request, symptoms, service type, urgency, preferred time, access notes, and relevant unit before dispatch. Support assessment or quote steps when the job needs them; carry approved scope and amounts forward into the job. | Office and field staff need a traceable request-to-work record without repeatedly retyping details. |
| R3 | Schedule and dispatch by branch, technician, date/time or arrival window, duration, priority, site, and work scope. Show assignment and status changes; prevent double-booking and disallow inactive or out-of-branch technicians. | Dispatch must reflect real technician capacity and keep each branch's operations in its own scope. |
| R4 | Give the technician a field-readable work order with customer/site/unit context, diagnosis, notes, inspection checklist, readings where relevant, parts/materials, and job status. Preserve incomplete, failed, and not-applicable checklist outcomes instead of silently treating them as passed. | The office needs a reliable record of what was inspected, found, and performed. Photos and customer sign-off are useful where the business chooses to collect them. |
| R5 | Use configurable, unit-appropriate aircon checklists. Common inspection topics include filters, coils, airflow, condensate drainage, outdoor-unit condition, thermostat/performance, electrical connections, and refrigerant/leak observations where the technician and equipment require them. | Manufacturer/DOE references describe overlapping checks, while also tying exact work to equipment type, model, and manufacturer instructions. |
| R6 | Track parts by branch, with item identity, unit, cost, on-hand/reorder levels, and append-only receiving/use/adjustment history. Attach consumed parts to the work order; guard stock updates against negative balances, retries, and concurrent use. | Stock needs to reconcile to jobs and costs; old movements should remain auditable. Purchase orders, suppliers, truck stock, barcode scanning, and transfers are larger extensions. |
| R7 | Carry approved service/material line items into an invoice. Record each payment as a separate dated event with method/reference and receipt; derive paid/partial/unpaid state and remaining balance from exact amounts. Keep invoice sending distinct from payment collection. | This preserves a correct money trail and makes partial balances and collection reports meaningful. Tax, discount, warranty, deposit, and credit policy must be configured by the business. |
| R8 | Retain completed-service history and explicitly saved next-maintenance dates. If the business adopts service agreements, support scheduled visits, reminders, and cancellations from those saved plans. | Follow-up should be based on an actual due date/agreement, not guessed from a universal interval. |
| R9 | Report jobs and collections by date, status, technician, and branch; show invoice balances/aging, parts usage/low stock, and maintenance due from stored records. | Managers need operational views that reconcile to source records rather than invented performance metrics. |
| R10 | Enforce tenant and branch authorization on every read/write; preserve audit history for consequential changes. Provide searchable, responsive, keyboard-usable screens with clear status text and loading, empty, error, and success feedback. | These are system trust and daily-use requirements, not optional visual polish. |

## End-to-end workflow and invariants

`Request (customer + site + unit + symptoms/urgency) → triage/assessment or quote → approved scope → scheduled work order → eligible technician dispatch → arrival/inspection/findings → authorized work + parts usage → completed work history → invoice → payment event(s) + receipt/balance → explicit follow-up date or service agreement.`

Keep the same records linked across status changes. Require valid lifecycle transitions; retain the branch/site/unit identity and an audit record; do not duplicate jobs on retry; do not decrement inventory twice; do not mark an invoice paid because it was sent; do not infer a payment amount from a legacy `PARTIAL` status. Cancellation/rescheduling must retain history and must not orphan assignments, charges, or receipts.

## Optional capabilities, only when the business selects them

- Public/customer portal and online service requests, quote approvals, and self-service payments.
- SMS/email notifications, arrival updates, automated quote/invoice follow-ups, or appointment reminders; each needs an approved provider and customer-contact/consent policy.
- Route/GPS optimization, technician time tracking, signatures, and job photo/document storage.
- Formal service agreements, recurring billing, seasonal/periodic job generation, and contract renewals.
- Supplier catalogs, purchase orders, receiving workflows, branch transfers, truck stock, and barcodes.
- Payment gateway, accounting integration, multi-party billing, job-costing/margin analysis, payroll, or financing.
- Technician qualification records and assignment restrictions where supported by confirmed local rules and business policy.

These are common product capabilities, but add provider costs, privacy/consent obligations, legal/accounting choices, or more complex workflows. They are not silently assumed by this implementation.

## Expanded operational and Philippine compliance research (checked 2026-10-06)

The checklist below expands the R1–R10 requirements into service-office and field steps. Product documentation is evidence of established workflows, not proof that every contractor needs every paid feature. Safety, refrigerant, tax, and privacy rules are distinguished from software choices.

### Service-management checklist

1. **Capture an inquiry before it is schedulable.** Record the contact/customer, callback details, service address, affected unit when known, reported symptoms, service type, urgency, access instructions, and preferred timing. Keep new/unconfirmed work visible without fabricating a scheduled appointment; allow an assessment, estimate, direct job, or close/archive path. Jobber documents internal request intake, unscheduled assessment work, request activity, and conversion to a quote or job. [Jobber Request Basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/request-basics/).
2. **Separate the customer, service location, and equipment.** A customer may have multiple sites and units per site. Retain each site's exact address/access notes and each unit's identity, manufacturer/model/serial where known, unit location, warranty/installation facts when supplied, and service history. Jobber supports location/property records; ServiceTitan defines a location as holding jobs, equipment, and history. These are product patterns, not legal definitions. [ServiceTitan Office Basics](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/office-basics), [ServiceTitan equipment records](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/manage-equipment-in-servicetitan-mobile).
3. **Prepare a traceable estimate when scope is uncertain.** Identify proposed labor/materials, quantities, user-entered prices, scope, exclusions, validity, and approval/decline/revision state. Carry the accepted scope forward rather than retyping it. Pricing, discounts, taxes, warranty, deposit, and credit terms come from ARCTIC's policy; do not use a universal catalogue or tax rate by inference. [Jobber Quote Basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quote-basics/).
4. **Dispatch from a live, branch-aware schedule.** Show unassigned work, assigned technician, appointment window, duration, priority, service location, and job context. Validate active technician, branch access, and overlapping scheduled work; support reassignment/rescheduling with a recorded reason. Route optimization, travel-time estimates, shifts, leave, and GPS are scale-dependent extensions. [ServiceTitan dispatch documentation](https://www.servicetitan.com/industries/hvac-software/dispatching), [Jobber HVAC work orders](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/hvac-work-order-software/).
5. **Give field staff a usable work order.** Surface customer/contact, service site/access notes, unit and prior history, reported issue, approved scope, checklist, notes, schedule, and relevant parts. Capture diagnosis, actual work, findings/readings, follow-up recommendations, and parts used. A sync queue for low-connectivity work is useful, but should not be implemented without explicit conflict, retry, and failure feedback. [Jobber HVAC work orders](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/hvac-work-order-software/).
6. **Use job- and unit-appropriate inspection forms.** Let authorized staff define which checks apply to their service types and equipment. Preserve pending, passed, failed/follow-up, and not-applicable outcomes with notes; capture measurements as values plus units when technicians choose them. Manufacturer instructions and qualified ARCTIC procedures determine checks and limits; no universal pressure, temperature-split, or service interval is assumed. [Jobber Checklists](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/checklists/), [Carrier HVAC maintenance guidance](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/residential/hvac-resources/hvac-maintenance/).
7. **Record parts movements and job consumption.** Track branch stock, receipts, adjustments, job usage, unit cost, and an auditable movement history; retryable stock actions need idempotency and must not allow negative stock. Purchasing, suppliers, transfers, vehicle stock, and barcode receiving are optional extensions. [ServiceTitan parts and purchase orders](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/manage-parts-purchase-orders).
8. **Keep exceptions visible.** Provide explicit routes for reassessment, declined or revised estimates, unassigned work, rescheduling, cancellation/no-access, incomplete inspection, parts shortage, return work, callback, failed/partial payment, and unpaid invoices. Record what happened and the next action; do not represent a visit as complete solely because an appointment time passed. This is a workflow recommendation drawn from vendor records, not a legal status vocabulary.
9. **Close out the visit with a retained service record.** Preserve actual work performed, checklist outcomes, findings, consumed parts, any follow-up recommendation, completion time, and optional customer acknowledgment. Photos/signatures are useful when ARCTIC chooses a retention and consent policy; they are not assumed mandatory for every visit. [ServiceTitan mobile job completion](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/complete-a-job-in-servicetitan-mobile-1).
10. **Issue invoices and record receipts separately.** Build invoice lines from the approved scope or explicit user-entered charges. Store each receipt with exact decimal amount, date, method, reference, and balance; sending an invoice does not mark it paid. Preserve legacy partial-payment records whose actual paid amount is unknown. Philippine invoice type, fields, thresholds, and tax treatment depend on the seller's registration and transaction; an app PDF is not automatically a valid BIR invoice. [BIR RR No. 7-2024](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-%202024.pdf), [BIR RMC No. 77-2024](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2077-2024%20Digest.pdf).
11. **Manage the next obligation.** Record a next-service date only when an authorized technician or service agreement supplies it. Show maintenance due, open recommendations, callbacks, and balances. Recurring plans, contractual intervals, reminders, and member pricing need ARCTIC-approved terms and notification consent before automation.
12. **Protect customer and employee records.** Keep tenant/branch access on every query and mutation, use least-privilege roles, audit consequential changes, define retention, and protect data in transit and at rest. The Data Privacy Act rules require reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and technical safeguards, scaled to processing risk; they do not require a specific field-service audit-screen design. [NPC Data Privacy Act IRR](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/), [NPC Data Security guidance](https://privacy.gov.ph/data-security/).
13. **Report work that needs a decision.** At minimum surface new requests, estimates awaiting a response, unscheduled/late/rescheduled work, today's visits, incomplete findings, work completed but not billed, unpaid balances by age from invoice issue date, low stock/parts usage, callbacks, and explicitly stored maintenance due dates. Report totals should be derived from source records and branch filters.
14. **Keep outbound communications and automation deliberate.** Appointment, quote, invoice, payment, and maintenance messages are useful only with a selected provider, delivery/error handling, and customer contact/consent rules. GPS, portals, online payments, recurring billing, payroll, and accounting integrations are optional, paid or privacy-sensitive extensions rather than baseline requirements.

### Philippines-specific duties and conditional scope

| Area | Research finding | Product consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales invoices | BIR RR 7-2024 and RMC 77-2024 establish invoice rules; an official receipt is generally supplementary in the EOPT transition framework, with transition details and treatment depending on the transaction/seller. | Keep internal job/payment records distinct from a BIR-compliant invoice. Confirm ARCTIC registration and fields with its accountant/BIR before representing generated documents as compliant. |
| Electronic invoices | BIR RMC 98-2026, issued Sep 22, 2026, covers specified taxpayer classes (including small/medium/large e-commerce or internet-transaction taxpayers, LTS and large taxpayers, and users of qualifying CAS/CBA or other invoicing software). Covered taxpayers must issue e-invoices by Dec 31, 2026; an approved system, BIR Permit to Issue, and EIS certification are required. RR 26-2025 supplies the extension. Actual application to ARCTIC depends on its classification, channel, and invoicing setup. | Treat as a near-term accountant/BIR decision. Do not claim this app's invoices satisfy the mandate or emit e-invoices until ARCTIC's scope, approved software/system, PTI, EIS certification, and BIR data protocol are confirmed. Corrections must reference a separate document; downtime uses authorized manual invoices and later corresponding electronic invoices under RMC 98-2026. [RMC 98-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2098-2026%20redacted.pdf), [RR 26-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2026-2025%20Digest.pdf). |
| Refrigerant/service-provider registration | DAO 2013-25 Section 10 covers ODS-using equipment service-provider registration; DAO 2021-31 controls HFCs. EMB regional materials describe ODS/HFC equipment service-provider applications and credential/equipment documents. The relevant refrigerant and operating region matter. [DAO 2013-25](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/72145), [DAO 2021-31](https://pod.emb.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/DAO_2021-31_Chemical-Control-Order-for-HFCs.pdf), [EMB Region 8 Citizens Charter](https://r8.emb.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/EMB8-Citizens-Charter-External-Internal-Service-2025-Edition-portrait-1.pdf). | Ask EMB for current applicability, branch coverage, renewal, and required evidence for ARCTIC's actual work. Credential assignment gates need confirmed company/person records and should not be invented in demo data. |
| Workplace safety | RA 11058 requires applicable employer/contractor safety duties, hazard information, training, and hazard-appropriate PPE; the Act does not require a universal digital checklist in this application. [RA 11058](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/86355). | Provide configurable records only; ARCTIC's safety officer and qualified staff define site/job checks and PPE. Do not present generic form completion as legal certification. |
| Personal information | DPA IRR Rule VI requires reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and technical safeguards; lawful purpose, proportionality, and retention also matter. | Preserve authorization, minimize collected data, and decide retention/consent before adding photos, geolocation, or outbound marketing. |

## Decisions to keep explicit

- The user chose four demo branches: Makati, Quezon City, Cavite, and Bulacan. This does not authorize changing a production database or fabricating street addresses, branch leaders, technicians, or customer records.
- Define actual emergency/SLA policy, service catalog/prices, tax/discount/warranty/credit rules, required checklist items and measurement limits, service intervals/agreement coverage, notification channel/consent, payment/accounting providers, and any locally required technician credentials before enabling those policies.
- Carrier, DOE, U.S. EPA, and OSHA references are not applied as Philippine legal requirements. Use manufacturer documentation and the business's qualified technician/SOP for technical tasks and intervals.
